# The Illusion of Intelligence: The End of the Factory-Built Ability Evaluation System

## 1. The Origins of School Education

Modern school education was designed during the Industrial Revolution to produce factory workers. Arriving on time, following instructions, moving as a group — these were the skills factories needed. The remnants of this system remain unchanged in today’s schools.

## 2. Who Defined “Intelligence”?

The abilities schools have measured are primarily memory, calculation, and the speed to solve predetermined problems. These have been defined as “intelligence” and “academic ability.” However, this definition was nothing more than a selection criterion optimized for factory production. Schools have been evaluating the whole of human beings by cutting out only a portion of their abilities.

## 3. What AI Has Proven

The emergence of AI has revealed the contradictions in this structure. Memory, calculation, logical thinking — the abilities humanity spent thousands of years worshipping as the “pinnacle of intelligence” turned out to be exactly what AI does best. In other words, humanity has been worshipping as “intelligence” the very things AI can do most easily. Now that machines have proven they can replace the abilities factories needed, those criteria no longer hold meaning.

## 4. What School Education Has Been Destroying

Ironically, those whom schools judged as “unable children” may have possessed the very abilities needed in the AI era. The abilities school education has most neglected are the essence of true intelligence:

- The ability to question premises
- The ability to formulate new questions
- Understanding others by becoming them
- The ability to respond to unknown situations
- The ability to judge for oneself what one needs

These are precisely what AI struggles with most, and what school education has neglected most.

## 5. Empathy as Conformity Pressure

The same problem exists in the “empathy” schools have taught. Empathy contains self-bias. Therefore, only those close to the average are empathized with. It functions as a system that favors the majority and excludes minorities. True understanding of others means becoming that person. This cannot be taught — it can only grow through the accumulation of experience and self-reflection.

## 6. The Contradiction Between Morality and Evaluation

Schools teach “everyone is different and that’s okay” in moral education, while simultaneously creating a class system by ranking students by grades. This is a fundamental contradiction. No one knows what “superior ability” truly means. Yet schools rank people by a narrow set of abilities, and those rankings determine life opportunities. As a result, the class system based on factory-era values is reproduced. Children sense this contradiction acutely, and distrust of morality is born.

## 7. School Education Should Be Optional

The proposal is not to abolish school education, but to make it optional. Group living, social experience, basic academic skills — anyone who feels they need these can choose to learn them. Those who can learn on their own do not need to go. There is no reason to force anyone.

## 8. A New Proposal for Ability Evaluation

Stop ranking people. Instead, simply visualize characteristics across multiple axes. Map each person’s traits across diverse dimensions such as memory, calculation, creativity, understanding of others, and the ability to formulate questions. See these as characteristics, not superiority or inferiority. Use them as tools for self-understanding, not for comparison with others.

Deviation scores are about comparison and ranking against others. Graphs are about visualizing one’s own characteristics. This difference determines whether a class system is created or not.

## Conclusion

The emergence of AI has revealed the true nature of what humanity worshipped as “intelligence.” It was nothing more than a selection criterion created for factory production. Now is the time to break free from that illusion and rebuild the standards of ability evaluation from the ground up — toward a society where people with diverse characteristics can live by their own strengths.
